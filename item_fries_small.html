What Makes McDonald's Fries Different?

McDonald's fries are made from Russet Burbank and Shepody potatoes, cut into thin strips, partially fried at the factory, frozen, then fried again at the restaurant. They are seasoned with salt after frying. The ingredient list includes natural beef flavor (from hydrolyzed wheat and milk), dextrose for consistent golden color, and sodium acid pyrophosphate to prevent graying. The double-frying process creates the signature crispy outside and fluffy inside that has made these fries famous since 1955.

Nutrition Facts

Fries Size Comparison

Small Fries are the best choice if you want the fries taste without excess calories. The jump from small to medium adds 90 calories and costs $1.20 more — that is a steep per-calorie premium. Large fries are actually the best value per calorie at $0.65 per 100 cal, but they pack 490 calories. If you are watching calories, stick with small.

Are McDonald's Fries Vegan?

In the US, no. McDonald's fries contain natural beef flavor derived from milk and wheat. In the UK and some other countries, the fries use a different recipe and are vegan. If you follow a strict vegan or dairy-free diet in the US, McDonald's fries do not qualify. See our vegan options guide for alternatives.

Best Deals on Fries

The McDonald's app frequently runs a "free fries with any purchase" deal, often on Fridays — making the fries effectively free. Small fries are included in the $5 Meal Deal with a sandwich, 4-piece McNuggets, and a small drink. A Small Fries paired with a McDouble ($2.89) gives you a full meal for $4.48 — hard to beat at any fast food chain.
