How the McDonald's Fries and Sides Menu Works

The McDonald's fries and sides menu currently tracks four items across two product types: World Famous Fries in small, medium, and large, and Apple Slices as the only non-fried side option. That's a deliberately lean category — McDonald's keeps the sides list tight, with the real variety happening in the main item and drink categories.

How Sides Fit Into the Wider Menu

Fries are the default side inside every Extra Value Meal — medium fries are bundled into every combo at no extra charge beyond the meal price. Apple Slices appear as an alternative side in Happy Meals, where parents can swap fries for slices on kids' orders.

The key distinction: Fries are a meal component first and a standalone side second. Apple Slices are a standalone snack or Happy Meal swap. Neither competes with the other — they serve different ordering needs.

World Famous Fries: Prices, Calories, and Size Comparison

World Famous Fries are the most ordered item at McDonald's globally — and the most consequential side decision on the McDonald's fries and sides menu. The size you choose affects both your calorie total and your spend more than any other single decision in this category.

What the Numbers Actually Mean

The medium is the sweet spot on pure value math. You pay $0.50 more than a small and get 90 extra calories and meaningfully more volume. The large adds another $1.00 over the medium for 160 more calories — a larger jump in both price and calories.

The calorie gap is significant: A large is 250 kcal heavier than a small. If you're pairing fries with a Double Quarter Pounder with Cheese (740 kcal) and a regular soda (up to 280 kcal), the size of your fries is the single biggest lever left to adjust your total calorie count.

What Makes World Famous Fries Different

McDonald's fries are cooked in a blend of oils and seasoned with salt. They're made from Russet Burbank, Shepody, and other potato varieties, cut to a specific thickness, and par-fried before being finished at each restaurant. That process produces the consistent texture and flavor that makes them one of the most recognized fast food items in the world.

Important allergen note: McDonald's World Famous Fries in the USA contain natural beef flavor derived from wheat and milk derivatives. They are not suitable for strict vegetarian, vegan, or dairy-free diets. Always verify with the nutrition and allergen guide before ordering if dietary restrictions apply.

Where Apple Slices Fit

At 15 calories and $1.50, Apple Slices are primarily a Happy Meal side swap — parents can choose them instead of small fries when building a kids' meal. They also work as a standalone snack for anyone who wants something light without the calorie load of fries.

The calorie comparison is stark: A serving of Apple Slices at 15 kcal vs. a small fries at 230 kcal. If you're ordering for a child or watching your calorie intake, Apple Slices deliver a genuinely meaningful reduction — not a token one.

Apple Slices vs. Small Fries: Which Makes Sense?

  • Choose Apple Slices if you want the lowest-calorie side available, you're building a Happy Meal for a child, or you want a fruit-forward snack alongside a heavier main item.

  • Choose Small Fries if you want a savory side, you're pairing with a lighter sandwich like the McChicken or Filet-O-Fish, or you're adding a side to a standalone order.

Neither is the wrong choice — they serve different appetites and different ordering contexts.

Fries Inside Meals vs. Standalone: Value Breakdown

One of the most practical questions on the McDonald's fries and sides menu is whether to order fries standalone or get them bundled inside a combo. The answer almost always favors the combo.

The Math on Medium Fries

Scenario Cost of Medium Fries
Standalone medium fries ~$3.79
Inside a Big Mac Meal (~$9.69) Effectively ~$1.50–$2.00 when meal savings are factored in
Inside a McValue bundle Often cheaper still

Ordering medium fries standalone at $3.79 is the most expensive way to get them. Inside an Extra Value Meal, the fries are bundled with a sandwich and drink at a total price that typically saves $1.50–$2.50 over ordering all three items separately. The fries are effectively the item that absorbs most of that discount.

When Standalone Makes Sense

  • You only want fries — no sandwich, no drink

  • You're adding a side to an existing order that didn't include fries

  • You're ordering a McValue menu item that doesn't bundle fries

  • You want a different size than what's included in your meal (e.g., upgrading to large outside the combo)

App deals note: The McDonald's app frequently offers free or discounted fries as standalone promotions — particularly the small size. Check the deals and McValue guide before ordering fries at full price. A free small fries offer can make the standalone math work in your favor.

Size Upgrade Strategy: When to Go Large

Inside an Extra Value Meal, the default fries size is medium. Most locations offer a medium-to-large upgrade for approximately $1.00. Whether that's worth it depends on your calorie budget and how hungry you are.

The Upgrade Math

Medium Fries Large Fries Difference
Standalone price ~$3.79 ~$4.79 +$1.00
Calories 320 kcal 480 kcal +160 kcal
Volume Standard ~50% more Significant

When the Large Upgrade Makes Sense

  • You're sharing fries with someone else at the table

  • You're ordering a lighter main item (McChicken, Filet-O-Fish, McCrispy Strips) and have calorie headroom

  • You're already spending $10+ on the combo and the $1.00 is marginal

  • You know from experience that a medium isn't enough

When to Stay at Medium or Go Small

  • You're pairing with a high-calorie burger like the Double Quarter Pounder Meal — the large fries push the meal total to ~1,490 kcal

  • You're calorie-conscious and the 160 kcal difference matters

  • You're ordering for kids — the Happy Meal uses small fries by default, and that's appropriate for the portion size

Bottom line: The large upgrade at $1.00 is good value per calorie and per volume. But it's only worth it if you'll actually eat the extra fries. Upsizing and leaving food is the worst value outcome in this category.

How to Choose the Right Side from the McDonald's Fries and Sides Menu

The right side depends on your order context, calorie target, and whether you're inside a combo or ordering standalone.

By Calorie Goal

Lowest possible Apple Slices 15 kcal

Standard meal side Medium Fries 320 kcal
Maximum volume Large Fries 480 kcal

By Order Context

  • Inside an Extra Value Meal: Medium fries are already included. Upgrade to large for $1.00 if you want more volume. Downgrade to small at some locations if you want fewer calories.

  • Happy Meal order: Small fries or Apple Slices — both are standard options. Apple Slices are the better nutritional pick for kids.

  • Standalone snack: Small fries at ~$3.29 or Apple Slices at ~$1.50. Apple Slices win on price and calories; small fries win on satiety.

  • Sharing: Large fries at ~$4.79 for the table is almost always better value than two smalls at ~$6.58 combined.

McDonald's Fries and Sides Menu: Frequently Asked Questions

What is on the McDonald's fries and sides menu?

A small World Famous Fries has 230 calories, a medium has 320 calories, and a large has 480 calories. The 250-calorie gap between small and large makes size choice the single biggest calorie lever in this category.
